### Level 1 Spellcasting
As a practice of Necromancy, you have a grimoire containing spells and incantations from the world beyond 
#### Cantrips

At 1st level, you know 2 cantrips of your choice from the Necromancy spell list. You learn additional Necromancy cantrips of your choice at higher levels, as shown in the Cantrips Known column of the Necromancy table.
#### Spell book

At 1st level, you have a spell book containing three 1st-level Necromancer spells of your choice. Your spell book is the repository of the wizard spells you know, except your cantrips, which are fixed in your mind.
##### Preparing and Casting Spells
:
The Necromancer table shows how many spell slots you have to cast your wizard spells of 1st level and higher. To cast one of these spells, you must expend a slot of the spell's level or higher. You regain all expended spell slots when you finish a long rest.
:
You prepare the list of Necromancer spells that are available for you to cast. To do so, choose a number of Necromancer spells from your spell book equal to your Intelligence modifier + your wizard level (minimum of one spell). The spells must be of a level for which you have spell slots.
:
For example, if you're a 3rd-level Necromancer, you have four 1st-level and two 2nd-level spell slots. With an Intelligence of 16, your list of prepared spells can include six spells of 1st or 2nd level, in any combination, chosen from your spell book. If you prepare the 1st-level spell magic missile, you can cast it using a 1st-level or a 2nd-level slot. Casting the spell doesn't remove it from your list of prepared spells.
:
You can change your list of prepared spells when you finish a long rest. Preparing a new list of Necromancer spells requires time spent studying your spell book and memorizing the incantations and gestures you must make to cast the spell: at least 1 minute per spell level for each spell on your list.
#### Spellcasting Ability

Intelligence is your spellcasting ability for your Necromancer spells, since you learn your Necromancer spells through communion with the dead of old. You use your Intelligence whenever a spell refers to your spellcasting ability. In addition, you use your Intelligence modifier when setting the saving throw DC for a wizard spell you cast and when making an attack roll with one.
- Spell save DC = 8 + Intelligence modifier + Proficiency Bonus
- Spell attack modifier = Intelligence modifier + Proficiency Bonus
#### Ritual Casting

You can cast a Necromancer spell as a ritual if that spell has the ritual tag and you have the spell in your spell book. You don't need to have the spell prepared.
Spellcasting Focus
:
You can use an organ bag as a spellcasting focus for your Necromancer spells.
#### Learning Spells of 1st Level and Higher
:
Each time you gain a Necromancer level, you can add two Necromancer spells of your choice to your spell book. Each of these spells must be of a level for which you have spell slots, as shown on the Necromancer table. On your adventures, you might find other spells that you can add to your grimoire (see "Your Spell book").

### Level 2 Summon Undead
As a Necromancer you have gained the ability to raise undead creatures to serve your bidding. 

As an action raise undead of challenge rating 1/4 or lower. You must pay half the creature your summons’s health in unmitigated max health damage to yourself. When the summon is destroyed or dismissed you regain the max hp lost, but not the hp itself. You must remain within 60ft of you summon for them to remain active. If you go unconscious while having active summons the summons disappears. Summons act on your initiative.

You can summon higher challenge rating undead as you level up according to the following table

#### Reanimated Champion 
Necromancers choosing the focus of reanimated champion have a deep relationship with a lost ally whom they wish to give a second chance at life , or wish to revive multiple dead to aid on their quest.
:
When choosing a champion permanently reduce your maximum health by half. Depending on the type of champion you choose it gains the following benefits. If your champion is of cr rating 1 or lower. Your champion takes on the amount of Hp you have given up. Your champion has the same attribute spread you do, but can be in different attributes. Your champion has the weight, speed, height, etc. that they did before death. Additionally, they will pick a class and gain the features of that class at level up as if they were a pc . If your champion of cr 2 or higher. Your champion takes on the amount of Hp you have given up. Your champion has the same attribute spread they had on death. Your champion has the weight, speed, height, etc. that they did before death. They retain whatever class features they had on death, and get no additional features. Champions of Cr 1 and lower will be called Origin Champions
:
Both Champion types must remain in the same dimension as you else they will become inanimate until you return.
:
Your Champion functions independently from you. They do not need to eat, or sleep. They retain all of their memories before death. If you fall unconscious in battle your champion can still function normally. If your champion loses all of their hp they will fall unconscious, but not die outright. They will reawaken after 8 hours or when healed. If you die outright your champion will die with you. You may dismiss your champion to choose a new one, but you will not recover your max hp. If you choose to revive a champion that is not your ally they must fail a charisma saving throw on their turn or they can act independently of your commands.

##### Origin Champion
Your Origin champion can select any one of the original dnd classes or homebrew class if it fits their narrative purpose. 

#### Rite of Undeath
 Necromancers who have performed the rite of undeath have separated soul from body. This rite has turned them undead which comes with both boons and curses. 
 
##### Soul Cage
In completing the rite, you have received a soul cage. It contains your soul. You may choose to keep your soul on your person, or stow it away somewhere. However, the one who holds your soul holds power over you.

##### Undead
While undead you cannot die outright. If you fall unconscious you will still perform death saves. If you succeed on all 3 saves you raise up instantly with 1 hp. If you fail all three saves and your body is intact you will revive where you feel in 8 hours. If your body is destroyed a new body will form over the course of a week where your soul is located.
:
Normal healing magic will damage you while undead, but you absorb all necrotic damage dealt to you. You have resistance to cold damage, and vulnerability to fire damage. You don’t need to eat, drink, breath etc. You can speak to the dead and undead freely regardless if the undead knows your language. If you are undead your summons will not die if you go unconscious provided you have at least 1 max hp.

You gain additional features as an undead on subsequent subclass level ups.

### Level 5 Drain Life
At level 5 you gain access to new spell drain. This spell does not count against your total prepared spell list.

#### Drain
*1th-level necromancy*

**Casting Time:** :: 1 action
**Range:**        :: 30 feet
**Components:**   :: S, V, M ()
**Duration:**     :: Until Broken

Pick a living creature and perform a spell attack against them. On hit a soul chain between you and the creature. Drain and deal 3d8 necrotic damage from them and add it to your hp bar. If your already at your hp maximum add the remainder to your temp hp You cannot gain more temp hp than half your maximum. On subsequent turns gain drain an additional 2d8 hp provided the soul chain remains intact. The soul chain will break if the chained creature dies, moves further than 30ft from you, is affected by dispel magic, or uses their action to perform an intelligence saving throw against your save dc to dispel the chain. You can only have one active link at a time.

You can use a higher-level spell slot to add an additional 1d8 to the initial attack.
